Here’s a **selection of recent (2024–2025) research that would be directly relevant to Moira’s course**, especially for Projects 4 and 5 — focusing on **AI persona design**, **text–image multimodality**, **generative iteration systems**, and **multimodal dialogue/creativity**. Where possible I’ve chosen *arXiv titles* or open-access reviews so students can actually read them.

---

### 🧠 **AI Persona & Voice (Project 4 relevance)**

**Systematizing LLM Persona Design: A Four-Quadrant Technical Taxonomy for AI Companion Applications**

* A comprehensive taxonomy for how “persona” is technically constructed and maintained in LLM systems — including emotional consistency, engendered narratives, and interaction modalities. Relevant to theorizing *Assistant Axis* and persona drift as an engineered phenomenon rather than a stable property. ([arXiv][1])

**How Is Generative AI Used for Persona Development?**

* A systematic review of how LLMs are applied in different stages of persona creation (data, segmentation, enrichment, evaluation). Useful for thinking about *how* character models are made and evaluated. ([arXiv][2])

**A Systematic Review on AI-Based Multimodal Dialogue Systems Capable of Emotion Recognition**

* Not arXiv, but a recent 2025 pub review about multimodal dialogue and emotion recognition — helpful for linking bots’ emergent sociality with affective indexing in multimodal interaction. ([MDPI][3])

---

### 🎨 **Multimodal Generation & Text↔Image Systems (Project 5 relevance)**

**Comparison of Text-Based and Image-Based Retrieval in Multimodal RAG Systems (2025)**

* Presents cutting-edge work on how multimodal RAG systems preserve or lose visual context when converting images → text representations — useful for understanding differences between text-centric vs. image-centric generative workflows. ([arXiv][4])

**Generative AI in Multimodal User Interfaces (2024)**

* Survey of how generative AI reshapes multimodal interaction — particularly relevant if students explore *new tools* that integrate text, voice, and image in fluid ways. ([arXiv][5])

---

### 📸 **Text-to-Image & Creative Iteration**

While slightly older or broader, these still help situate students’ work with diffusion and multimodal generators:

**Text-to-image Diffusion Models in Generative AI: A Survey (2024 version)**

* A major survey of how text prompts become images in diffusion systems — helps ground what students are doing when they iterate ekphrasis → image → description. ([arXiv][6])

**RePrompt: Automatic Prompt Editing to Refine AI-Generative Art Towards Precise Expressions (2023)**

* Not arXiv but widely cited; explores *prompt refinement* and emotional expressiveness in generated images — dovetails with iteration/negation workflows. ([arXiv][7])

---

### 🎭 **Critical & Creative Contexts**

**Detecting AI-generated Artwork (2025)**

* Recent work on technical classification between human vs. AI art — helps frame questions of originality / authorship in Generative AI creative outputs. ([arXiv][8])

*(Not arXiv, but worth mentioning for humanities framing):*

**AI and Image: Critical Perspectives on the Application of AI in Art & Heritage (2025)**

* A critical humanities examination of how AI shapes visual culture, bias, and museum/heritage practices — excellent for tying tech to broader cultural critique. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment][9])

---

### 📚 Recommended for Background / Broader Students

These aren’t strictly arXiv but provide excellent context for class discussion and student reading lists:

* *Generative AI and Human Creativity*: evidence that text-to-image tools shift creative workflows and how novelty metrics behave. ([PMC][10])
* Refik Anadol’s generative art projects as case studies of large-scale multimodal AI art practice. ([Wikipedia][11])
* Jake Elwes’s projects that explicitly loop AI interpretations of text and images — great conceptual parallels to Project 5’s iterative loops. ([Wikipedia][12])

## 🧠 How these connect to the course

**For Project 4 (Monster Theatre & Persona):**

* *Systematizing LLM Persona Design* helps historicize persona engineering rather than treating human-like voice as magical. ([arXiv][1])
* Persona development reviews show how AI “characters” are constructed and evaluated. ([arXiv][2])

**For Project 5 (Imaginary Museum & Iteration):**

* Multimodal RAG, UI, and diffusion surveys give students conceptual and technical backing for iterative pipelines. ([arXiv][4])
* Prompt refinement and style replication research help frame the *negation/iteration* method as situated in current creative AI scholarship. ([arXiv][7])
